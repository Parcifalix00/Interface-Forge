# Color, typography, and application

Propose directions based on audience, purpose, and available material. Do not automatically equate premium with serif, technology with neon, or dashboards with dark mode. Keep a functional rationale rather than an arbitrary aesthetic score.

Brand colors are not the complete semantic color system. Define real text/background pairs and state colors without overwriting the brand to solve one contrast issue. Evaluate surfaces, functional tones, and context.

For web use, consider at least 4.5:1 for ordinary text and 3:1 for large text under WCAG definitions and exceptions. UI components and necessary graphical information require the specific non-text contrast criterion. Do not apply these thresholds to every decorative pixel.

Use existing fonts or a proposal with a verifiable name and source. Check required language characters, accents, numerals, weights, fallbacks, and licensing. Do not attach or redistribute font files. A preview should distinguish the font actually rendered from a fallback.

Validate logos at the minimum intended size, on real backgrounds, in monochrome when required, and without distortion. Preserve official SVG and proportions. Do not redraw an official mark with a generator merely to create a layout variant.

Sources: [WCAG contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html), [non-text contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).
